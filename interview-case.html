Start with the user

Someone just bought a home. They open Boligmappa to find their documents… and they can’t get in.

Or worse: they still see the previous owner’s folder — or nothing at all. That’s the case.

The problem, in one sentence

After a home changes hands, the right person often couldn’t access their digital home folder.

Boligmappa is Norway’s “digital binder” for a property — papers, history, access for the owner. When ownership changes in real life, our product often lagged behind.

Why users care

This isn’t a data bug. It’s a blocked life moment.

I need my papers

Insurance, renovation, selling again — people expect the folder to follow the home.

I feel locked out

“I own this place — why doesn’t the app know?” Trust drops fast.

Support becomes the product

Users call. Agents dig in databases. Slow, stressful, expensive.

Picture it

What “broken” looked like

Before

Home is sold → new owner logs in → folder missing, wrong, or still tied to the seller. Nobody has a clear “source of truth” for who should have access now.

Goal

Home is sold → system updates from official ownership data → new owner gets in, old owner loses access, support isn’t the workaround.

Why this was hard

Three reasons it wasn’t a simple sync

  • 1 Official data isn’t always complete or timely — so blind auto-updates can lock the wrong people out.
  • 2 Homes aren’t always “one owner, one house” — shares, companies, co-owners make access messy.
  • 3 Mistakes hurt more than delays — removing the right owner by accident is worse than being a day late.

What we did

We made access updates safe enough to ship

1. See the mismatch clearly

First: show “who the official register says” vs “who has access today” — so we stop guessing.

2. Automate only the safe cases

Clear person → clear home → update. Ambiguous cases get flagged, not silently broken.

3. Ship in slices

Start with the common “sold to a person” path users hit most — then expand.

4. Measure with real pain

Success = fewer locked-out buyers and less support archaeology — not “% of rows synced”.
